# Progressive Probing Protocol

Depth means evidence and decision pressure, not a sequence of definition questions.

## Probe ladder

1. `fact`: What happened and what did you do?
2. `mechanism`: Why did the approach work?
3. `ownership`: Which decisions and execution were yours?
4. `alternative`: What else did you consider?
5. `tradeoff`: What did the chosen approach sacrifice?
6. `failure`: Where does it break and how did you handle it?
7. `measurement`: What were the baseline, window, metric, and attribution method?
8. `counterfactual`: If a key condition changed, would the decision still hold?

Choose the order that fits the Claim; do not mechanically ask all eight.

## Adaptive branch

- **A**: credible, specific, owns trade-offs. Move to counterfactual or a harder adjacent constraint.
- **B**: sound core with one material gap. Probe that gap once more.
- **C**: incomplete, generic, or weakly evidenced. Name the missing proof, give no ideal answer, and request a focused retry.
- **D**: contradictory, fabricated-sounding, or unable to explain the Claim. Give the minimum concept needed, narrow the Claim if appropriate, then retry before scoring.

Style pressure and score rigor are independent. Friendly mode does not lower the evidence bar.

## Stop conditions

Stop the chain when:

- required probes are covered and the next question has low information value;
- a contradiction requires the candidate to verify facts outside the session;
- the chain is becoming domain trivia unrelated to the claimed work;
- the candidate has failed twice and a targeted drill is higher leverage than continued pressure.

## Recording

For each unit record sequence, parent sequence, probe type, depth, linked Claim IDs, answer summary, evidence, and A–D grade. The human-facing debrief continues to use the five core dimensions.
